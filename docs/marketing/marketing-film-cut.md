## Narration — timed to the segments above

**1–3 · the problem (21 s)**

> There is always a list.
> The things you mean to get to. The run you were going to start.
> The instrument you were going to pick back up.
> They do not fail loudly. They just never get a time.

**4 · smart add (19 s)**

> So start where you actually are — a sentence.
> Say what you want to do, the way you would say it to a friend.
> GoalPilot reads it, files it under the right goal, and shows you where it went
> before it saves a thing.

**5 · the estimate (10.5 s)**

> It works out how demanding it is, how long it will take, and what it is worth.
> You are not guessing at your own week any more.

**6–7 · the calendar (22.5 s)**

> Then it stops being a list and becomes time.
> Real hours, on real days, with the load you are actually carrying.
> Miss one? Pick it up and put it where it should have been.

**8 · life areas (8.7 s)**

> Because a goal is not a chore. It belongs to a part of your life you decided was worth
> investing in.

**9 · analytics (17 s)**

> And at the end of it, the honest answer.
> Not a score. Not a grade. Just where your time actually went —
> including the part that was never filed at all.

**10–12 · the payoff (24 s)**

> The list does not need more discipline.
> It needs a time.
> **GoalPilot. Give it a time.**

## Voice notes

- One voice, warm, unhurried, mid-pace. Not an ad-read.
- The three problem lines and the three payoff lines want space around them; the product
  section can run closer together.
- ⚠️ Never call `#64`'s run a **score**, a **rate**, or describe it as **red** — the narration
  above deliberately says *"Not a score. Not a grade."*
