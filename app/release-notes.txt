GoalPilot 0.5.0 — a new goal now files itself, and offers you a plan

This is the release where creating a goal stops being a form you fill in and starts
being a conversation. Two things are new, and the second one is the big one.

1. THE GOAL FILES ITSELF

Type a title and press Create. The app reads the title and puts the goal under the
right life area and the right category on its own — you do not pick them.

"Run a marathon successfully" goes to Health, as Fitness.

It is silent on purpose: filing a goal is bookkeeping, not a decision about your
life, and it is one tap to change if it gets it wrong. If you DO pick a life area
yourself — including tapping "None" — the app leaves your choice alone and does not
second-guess it.

2. A WORK PLAN YOU APPROVE, THAT LANDS ON YOUR CALENDAR

Straight after the goal is created, a sheet slides up with a proposed plan: real
steps, with real dates, spread over real weeks. For "Run a marathon successfully"
that is around ten items over about three months — easy runs, long runs, a 10K, a
half marathon, and the marathon itself.

Every step has a tick. Untick anything you do not want. Press "Add N steps" and
what is left becomes:

  - tasks on the goal, each with its own date and duration
  - entries on GoalPilot's own Calendar screen
  - events in the GOALPILOT calendar inside Google Calendar, if you have connected it

Nothing is written until you press that button. The plan is a proposal until then,
and closing the sheet writes nothing at all.

Two details worth knowing. Milestones — "Run 20 miles", "Finish marathon" — are
states you reach, so they get a date but no duration and no points: they are not
work you sit down and do. And work with a time of day is placed as a provisional
block, which means if you miss it, the app says nothing. A plan you accepted and
then did not follow is not going to turn into a wall of failures.

You can also ask for a plan on a goal you already have: open it for editing and
press "Suggest a work plan".

ALSO IN THIS BUILD

The visual work that has been landing over the last day comes with it — the
glassmorphism look is now the default rather than something you had to turn on, and
screens animate in. Challenge scoring now reads from your goals instead of waiting
for someone to type a number, and it says who reported a score when a person did.

KNOWN, AND SAID OUT LOUD

The plan call fails roughly one time in six, and when it does the sheet tells you so
and offers a retry. Press it again — it works. That is the free AI tier being
unreliable rather than the app being broken, and it is being worked on.

The new sheet is in English even if the rest of your app is in Hebrew. The plan
STEPS themselves come back in your language — Hebrew goal, Hebrew steps — but the
buttons and headings around them are not translated yet.
