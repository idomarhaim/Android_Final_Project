## 4. "I don't understand the options" means the axis is not reduced far enough — corroboration, not a new claim

**Claim.** When a user answers a multi-option question with *"I could not understand
you or what each option implies"*, the productive response is **not** more explanation
inside the options — it is reducing the question to a smaller axis and re-asking. In
the source session the fix that worked, twice, was collapsing four prose-heavy options
into a **2×2 of who does which job**, stated before the picker rather than inside it.

**Why.** Two consecutive question pickers came back with the identical complaint. Both
were dense: multi-sentence option bodies, reasoning nested inside each choice, and
vocabulary inherited from the tickets. The recovery was to name the two sub-jobs the
question was really about (*"deciding what kind of thing, and deciding which one"*),
observe that each could be done by either party, and present the same four options as
a 2×2 table. **The options did not change; only their presentation did** — which is
the evidence that the problem was framing, not content. Every subsequent picker was
short and answered on the first attempt.

**Why this is filed as corroboration.** A parked candidate from the
`entity-model-intake` session, written a day earlier and still awaiting Ido, states a
stronger and more general version: *"'It doesn't understand me' usually means every
option shares a framing the user doesn't hold."* This session hit the same wall
independently, from a different task, and the recovery was consistent with that
diagnosis — the shared framing here was *"selection is one decision"*, and the 2×2
broke it into two. **Two independent sessions reaching the same finding is the
strongest argument for that entry**, so this should be ingested **into** it rather than
beside it.

**Destination.** Wherever the `entity-model-intake` parked entry lands.

the same thing".
