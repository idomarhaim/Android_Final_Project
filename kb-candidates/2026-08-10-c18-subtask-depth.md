## Entry 1 — the granularity failure mode, observed live: a picker names the axis correctly and Ido answers with a *unit*

**Status:** ⛔ **always-ask, not ingested.** Destination is `rules/`, which `/kb-ingest`
may not take in either mode, and it is an **amendment to a live rule**
(`C:\Dev\JARVIS\rules\question-axis-naming.md` is shipped and in force). It is also the
**second sighting** of a mode already parked by another session — so this entry is
*evidence for* that parked candidate, not a rival to it.

**Claim.** The parked fourth picker-axis failure mode — **granularity**, where *the axis
is right as a dimension and wrong as a unit, and whose tell is that Ido answers
**fluently** with something not on the menu* — reproduced in a second, independent
session on an unrelated subject. That moves it from one observation to a pattern, and
supplies the missing half of its remedy.

**What happened, concretely.** `#39` asked for a depth limit on nested tasks. The picker
named the axis explicitly and said so in the question text: *"the axis here is not the
number — it's **who a limit binds**"*, offering *limit the agent not you* · *no limit on
either* · *a hard cap on both*, and naming two axes considered and dropped. Ido answered:

> **"מקסימום עומק 10"** — *maximum depth 10.*

Fluent, instant, unhesitating — and **not on the menu**. He answered in the unit (a
number) the question had explicitly ruled out as the axis. He was not confused and did
not stall; he simply had an answer at a granularity the picker had reasoned its way past.

**Why this matters, and what it adds to the parked entry.** The parked sighting recorded
the tell. This one shows the axis-naming discipline **does not prevent it** — the axis
was named, the dropped axes were listed, and it happened anyway. So the remedy is not
*name the axis harder*. The candidate remedy this sighting supports:

> When the picker's own question text says *"the axis is not X, it's Y"* — and **X is a
> quantity the user could simply state** — offer X as an option anyway, or expect the
> answer in X. A dimension the agent reasons past is often the one the user has already
> priced. The reasoning that promoted Y over X is genuine analysis and belongs in the
> reply; it is not grounds for making X unsayable.

**And the second half, which is the recoverable part:** when the off-menu answer lands,
**the granularity gap is usually derivable, and the derivation must be shown, not
silently taken.** Here *"depth 10"* left one thing open — 10 levels of *what*, given that
`C16` made objectives nest too and left their depth undecided. That was resolved by
derivation (10 along the whole goal → milestone → task chain, since a roll-up walks one
chain) and **shown to Ido flagged for override** rather than asked again. Re-asking would
have been the wrong move — he had answered; the answer just needed a scope.

**Why (what was rejected).** Rejected: treating the off-menu answer as a failed question
and re-popping a picker. My own rules already forbid that shape — *"when I say I don't
understand the options, make the question smaller — do not explain more"* — and this was
the opposite case: Ido understood perfectly and answered past the menu. Re-asking would
have read as not listening. Also rejected: recording this in the released
`c9c-calendar-sync` candidate file where the mode was first parked — that file belongs to
a session that has released, and appending to it would be writing outside this session's
paths.

**Destination.** `rules/question-axis-naming.md` — as an amendment to the granularity
mode, adding (a) the second sighting, (b) the finding that naming the axis does not
prevent it, and (c) the derive-and-show remedy for the scope the off-menu answer leaves
open.
